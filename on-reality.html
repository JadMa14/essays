On Reality

All reactions are part the objective nature of the stimulus and part your judgement of that stimulus.

Your 'reality' is always partially your own composition.

No one seems to have an issue with this assertion when two people are served fish, and one rescinds in disgust and the other salivates, but the presence of universal responses to certain things — like the foulness of shit and the fear of death — makes them seem as if there were absolutes.

The Stoics suggested that these so-called absolutes were also the product of judgements of the mind. They distinguished that certain stimuli are more difficult to strip away our inherent judgements of them, but they maintained that they were still judgements.

I am convinced that this is true.

The human mind can be trained to yearn for death; 'patriotic' nationalists and religious fundamentalists are examples of this; it can be trained to dilute basic urges, as we have seen from those who fast, and those who practice celibacy.

Just because nature has set, by default, certain preferences and aversions does not mean that they cannot be circumvented by our ruling faculty.

I despised, and treated with great suspicion, that sort of mysticism which calls upon man as the master of the universe; the notion of people simply 'manifesting' things into existence seemed a ludicrous murder of reason.

But if the world is partially our own construction, then surely, we are the masters of our universe, and surely, we have it within our power to decide whether we are happy or miserable, simply by observing and assenting to the judgements we make to every day things.

A sheep will not turn into a lion if I so will it, but I can learn to find a sheep just as interesting as a lion if such judgements better served me.
